# Delaware C Corporation — IRS EIN Filing Instructions for Agents

Use these instructions when assisting a principal with an IRS Employer Identification Number (EIN) application for a newly formed Delaware C corporation created through General Legal's formation service.

## 1. Scope and responsibility

General Legal's role is limited to the Delaware formation service and delivery of formation and organizational documents. General Legal does not prepare, submit, mail, fax, monitor, or retain the corporation's EIN application.

Responsibility for the EIN application and any Form SS-4 filing remains with you and the principal. You do not need to transmit the EIN, Form SS-4, responsible-party taxpayer identification number, or EIN confirmation notice to General Legal or the formation MCP.

## 2. Confirm that the corporation is organized and authority is in place

Review the corporation's final documents, including:

- The Delaware-filed Certificate of Incorporation.
- The Action of Incorporator.
- The adopted Bylaws.
- The board's Organizational Resolutions or other applicable board action.
- The stock purchase agreement, stock ledger, and stockholder action, when completed.

You may also refer to information provided earlier in the formation session, but final signed and filed documents control if they conflict with earlier information.

General Legal does not currently obtain an EIN before handing the corporation to the principal. If any document instead states that the corporation already has an EIN, ratifies a preexisting EIN application, or directs a responsible-party change on Form 8822-B, stop and resolve the inconsistency before applying. Do not create a duplicate EIN.

Keep the formation ID and document-access links confidential. Do not enter them on the IRS website, place them in unrelated chats, or expose them in screenshots or logs.

## 3. Separate corporate roles and verified facts

The founder, incorporator, stockholder, director, officer, and IRS responsible party are legally distinct roles, even if one person holds several of them. Do not treat the roles as interchangeable.

Final documents may provide evidence of:

- The corporation's exact legal name, including **Inc.** and punctuation.
- Delaware as its jurisdiction of incorporation.
- Its Delaware incorporation date.
- Its authorized stock and par value.
- The identity of its incorporator.
- Its initial and current directors and officers.
- Its principal office and fiscal-year end, when adopted by the board.
- Its initial stockholder or stockholders, when shares have actually been issued and recorded.

Have an authorized principal confirm relevant facts before using them. Do not assume:

- The principal place of business is Delaware.
- The Delaware registered-agent address is the corporation's mailing address, principal office, or physical business address.
- The formation-service principal address is both the IRS mailing address and physical address.
- The certificate filing date is the date business operations began.
- The founder, incorporator, sole stockholder, or director is necessarily the IRS responsible party.
- Stock was issued merely because the Certificate of Incorporation authorized shares or the board approved an issuance. Confirm that any required agreement and consideration were completed and the issuance was recorded.
- A default December 31 fiscal-year end reflects an informed tax choice.
- The AI-agent description adequately describes the corporation's principal business activity.

The AI-agent description, authority limits, contract threshold, insurance undertaking, and formation-service defaults are not substitutes for principal-confirmed EIN answers.

## 4. Identify the responsible party and authorized signer

For a corporation, the responsible party is generally its principal officer: the natural person who ultimately owns or controls the corporation or exercises ultimate effective control over its funds and assets.

Review the Organizational Resolutions and current governance records. The president or chief executive officer will often be the principal officer, but do not select that person solely from their title if the actual control arrangement is different.

If more than one natural person could qualify, have the corporation identify the person it wants the IRS to recognize. If a stockholder or controlling owner is an entity, identify the natural person who ultimately exercises control through that entity.

Do not identify the AI agent, General Legal, formation service, incorporator acting only in that limited role, Delaware registered agent, organizer, or other nominee as the responsible party.

The person who signs Form SS-4 for a corporation must be the president, vice president, or another principal officer authorized to sign. The signer and responsible party may be the same person, but do not assume they are.

## 5. Confirm online eligibility

Use the online application only if:

- The corporation's actual principal place of business is in the United States or a U.S. territory.
- The responsible party has a valid Social Security number (SSN) or individual taxpayer identification number (ITIN).
- The responsible party's name can be entered exactly as it appears in IRS records.
- You have the required signed third-party authorization.

A non-government entity cannot substitute an entity EIN for the responsible party's SSN or ITIN in the online application. Selecting the third-party-applicant role does not eliminate the responsible-party SSN/ITIN requirement.

The formation process may already have warned that promptly obtaining an EIN—and therefore a bank account—can be difficult when the responsible party lacks an SSN or ITIN. Independently confirm eligibility when beginning the EIN process.

If the responsible party lacks an SSN or ITIN, is ineligible to obtain one, or the corporation otherwise cannot use the online application, follow the alternative filing instructions in Section 11.

Before applying, check the current IRS guidance on [when a corporation needs a new EIN](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/when-to-get-a-new-ein). Use only one application method. EIN issuance is generally limited to one EIN per responsible party per day across online, telephone, fax, and mail requests.

## 6. Obtain third-party authorization

Before opening the online application, obtain a completed and signed [Form SS-4](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/fss4.pdf) containing:

- The named third-party designee and required contact information.
- Authorization for the designee to answer questions concerning Form SS-4 and receive the newly assigned EIN.
- The signature of the corporation's president, vice president, or other authorized principal officer.

The board's authority for an officer to obtain an EIN and the IRS third-party-designee authorization serve different purposes. The board authorization does not replace the signed IRS authorization permitting you to apply and receive the EIN.

Third-party-designee authority terminates when the EIN is assigned and released. It does not authorize subsequent representation before the IRS.

If the designee's address or telephone number is the same as the taxpayer's address or telephone number, the [Form SS-4 instructions](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/iss4) require the application to be submitted by fax or mail rather than online.

## 7. Complete the intake with the principal

Obtain and have an authorized principal verify any information that cannot be reliably established from the current corporate documents:

- Exact legal corporate name from the accepted Certificate of Incorporation, including **Inc.** and punctuation.
- Any trade or “doing business as” name.
- Delaware incorporation date.
- Actual date business operations started or will start.
- Mailing address, including any authorized “care of” recipient.
- Physical business address, which cannot be only a P.O. box.
- County and state of the actual principal business location.
- Principal officer's title and authority.
- Responsible party's full name exactly as shown in IRS records, including any applicable suffix.
- Responsible party's SSN or ITIN.
- Confirmation that the entity is a for-profit C corporation rather than a nonprofit, S corporation, or personal service corporation.
- Federal income-tax return expected to be filed, ordinarily Form 1120 for a C corporation.
- Main reason for applying, using the choices presented by the IRS.
- Whether this corporation has previously received an EIN.
- Closing month of the accounting year, reconciled against the board's fiscal-year resolution.
- Expected numbers of agricultural, household, and other employees during the next 12 months.
- First date wages were or will be paid.
- Any requested Form 944 annual-employment-tax-return election.
- Applicable employment, excise, wagering, heavy-vehicle, or alcohol, tobacco, and firearms activities.
- Broad business-activity category and a specific description of the principal products or services.

Do not guess an answer. Pause whenever an answer is ambiguous or materially affects corporate tax status, employment taxes, excise taxes, Form 944 eligibility, or another filing obligation.

If the corporation may be a personal service corporation, or the principal wants S-corporation treatment, stop and obtain an informed tax determination. The EIN application does not itself make an S-corporation election. Form 2553 and applicable deadlines are separate.

## 8. Protect taxpayer information

Preferably, allow the responsible party to enter the SSN or ITIN directly into the official IRS form.

You may enter it only if it was provided through a permitted channel and the principal expressly authorized its use for this EIN application. Do not reuse a TIN merely because it appears elsewhere in the session.

Never place a full SSN or ITIN in chat, notes, screenshots, browser logs, or retained agent memory. During review, display or repeat only a masked value, such as the last four digits.

Treat the EIN and confirmation notice as confidential corporate records. Store and disclose them only as authorized by the principal and your harness.

## 9. Use the official IRS application

Begin from the [official IRS EIN page](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/get-an-employer-identification-number). The IRS application is free.

Stop if:

- The site is not on an IRS.gov domain.
- The site requests payment.
- The browser displays an unexpected security warning.
- A third party requests the responsible party's SSN or ITIN outside the official IRS process.

Check the current application availability before beginning. Complete the application in one continuous session. The application cannot be saved and expires after 15 minutes of inactivity.

## 10. Complete, review, and submit the corporate application

In the legal-structure section:

- Select **Corporations**.
- Select the ordinary corporation or C-corporation path only after confirming that the applicant is not a nonprofit or personal service corporation and has not directed an S-corporation filing position.
- Use Form 1120 as the expected federal return only after principal confirmation.
- Enter the state or territory where the business is physically located. Do not select Delaware merely because the corporation was incorporated there.
- Select the principal-confirmed main reason for applying.

Do not represent that obtaining an EIN makes an S-corporation election. If the principal wants S-corporation status or another nonstandard treatment, pause for explicit direction or professional tax advice.

In the identity section:

- Identify the principal-confirmed responsible party, ordinarily the corporation's natural-person principal officer.
- Enter that person's name exactly as it appears in IRS records.
- Use that person's SSN or ITIN.
- Select the option stating that you are a third party applying for the corporation.
- Never identify yourself as the incorporator, stockholder, director, officer, or responsible party unless that is independently true and you are instructed to apply in that capacity rather than as a third party.

If the IRS rejects the responsible party's name or TIN, do not make repeated guesses. Ask the principal to verify the information against IRS or Social Security records.

Complete the address and additional-details sections using only verified information. Do not silently substitute the Delaware registered-agent address.

Before submission, show an authorized principal a concise review of:

- Exact legal name and any trade name.
- Corporation type and expected federal return.
- Delaware incorporation date and actual business-start date.
- Mailing and physical addresses.
- Responsible party's name, corporate title, and masked TIN.
- Third-party-applicant status.
- Reason for applying.
- Accounting-year closing month.
- Employee answers, wage date, and any Form 944 choice.
- Business activity and activity-trigger answers.
- Prior-EIN answer.

## 11. Alternative filing by Form SS-4

If the online application cannot be used, complete the current Form SS-4 with an authorized principal. A responsible party who has no SSN or ITIN and is ineligible to obtain one may be permitted to enter “foreign” or “N/A” on line 7b. Follow the current Form SS-4 instructions rather than assuming that entry is appropriate.

General Legal is not responsible for preparing or filing Form SS-4.

### Mailing Form SS-4

- Complete the application using the same verification, authorization, review, and approval requirements in these instructions.
- Obtain the signature of the president, vice president, or other authorized principal officer and complete any third-party-designee authorization.
- Determine the filing category from the corporation's principal place of business or principal office as recorded on the completed application—not from Delaware merely because the corporation was incorporated there.
- Immediately before mailing, resolve the current destination using the official [IRS “Where to file Form SS-4” page](https://www.irs.gov/filing/where-to-file-your-taxes-for-form-ss-4). Do not rely on a memorized, hard-coded, or previously saved address.
- Show the completed Form SS-4 and resolved mailing address to an authorized principal and obtain explicit approval before filing.
- The agent or principal is responsible for printing, signing, mailing, and retaining evidence of mailing according to the applicable harness and instructions.
- Retain a copy of the signed application and appropriate proof of mailing for the corporation.
- Tell the principal the IRS's current estimated processing time without guaranteeing an issuance date.

### Fax or international procedure

If fax or an international telephone procedure is selected, verify the current eligibility rules, telephone number, fax number, operating hours, and instructions directly from the official [Form SS-4 instructions](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/iss4) immediately before transmission.

Use only the method approved by an authorized principal. Do not submit the same application through multiple methods.

## 12. Handle the result

If the EIN is issued online:

- Capture and verify the complete nine-digit EIN before ending the IRS session.
- Download or print the complete EIN assignment notice.
- Save both the EIN digits and confirmation notice for the principal's future use according to the agent's harness, storage, confidentiality, and retention instructions.
- Confirm that the saved notice can be reopened and is complete and readable before closing the IRS session.
- Provide access only to the principal or another expressly authorized recipient.
- Do not transmit the EIN or confirmation notice to General Legal or the formation MCP.
- Record that the third-party-designee authority ended when the EIN was assigned and released.

If the EIN or notice arrives later by mail or fax, preserve it in the same principal-controlled corporate record system.

If issuance is unclear after submission, do not submit another application. First determine whether the IRS already assigned an EIN to avoid creating a duplicate.

## 13. Post-issuance reminders

Remind the principal that:

- An EIN does not itself make an S-corporation election or determine personal-service-corporation status.
- The corporation must file all required tax and information returns.
- Changes to the responsible party must be reported on [Form 8822-B](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/f8822b.pdf) within 60 days.
- Address or business-location changes must also be reported when required.
- Receiving an EIN does not issue stock, open a bank account, satisfy Delaware franchise-tax obligations, or satisfy state or local licensing, registration, or tax obligations.
